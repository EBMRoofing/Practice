INTERNAL · THE THREE LEAKS — RANK THEM, OWN THEM, FIX THEM  (Signals #1–#3)
#1

Callback hours are this week's capacity destroyer

 ▲ LEAD SIGNAL
TAG: OPERATOR SIGNAL · UPGRADED FOCUS — see Featured Insight for the full math

Callbacks are not quality anecdotes — they are capacity destruction. Every callback hour a senior tech spends on rework is an hour not available for diagnostics, sold repairs, maintenance conversion, or replacement lead generation.

In a shoulder week, that leakage decides whether you convert HARDI/AHRI channel movement into GP dollars or spend the month treading water. Track hours per 100 calls — not count. One 20-minute return and one 5-hour mess should never be treated equally.

DO THIS →
Callback hours per 100 calls on the Monday dashboard, by tech/crew/cause/equipment type; senior techs on replacement diagnostics and repeat-call customers, not first-in-first-out; one senior-tech day per week reallocated to coaching the crews creating the most callback hours.
#2

Financing margin after fees is where GP dollars quietly disappear

 ▲ OPERATOR SIGNAL
TAG: OPERATOR SIGNAL — Backdrop: 30-yr 6.76% (9/10), up from 6.71% (9/3); 15-yr 6.09%

Most contractor content treats financing as pure sales support. It is also a margin risk. A financed close rate can climb while GP dollars per financed job fall — and no one notices until the monthly close. With rates back above 6.75% and homeowners payment-sensitive, the sales team needs to know financed margin after fees per lender, per plan, per advisor.

SAME $16,000 INSTALL · $12,000 EQUIPMENT COST · $4,000 GROSS PROFIT GP $ Δ VS CASH
Cash sale $4,000 —
Financed, 6% dealer fee ($960) $3,040 −24%
Financed, 9% dealer fee ($1,440) $2,560 −36%
Financed at 6% + priced to hold cash GP → sticker ~$17,020 $4,000 flat
Illustrative. Real dealer fees vary by lender, term, credit tier, and promotion. Substitute your own numbers — the pattern holds. Financed sales cost real GP dollars unless price or mix offsets the fee.

The diagnostic questions a veteran operator asks this week: is financing approval rate changing by lender? Are advisors moving to longer terms to hold the payment? Is the mix shifting down to lower equipment tiers? Are IAQ, accessory, and duct attach rates falling on financed sales? Are cash buyers behaving differently from financed buyers (close rate, average ticket, accessory attach)?

DO THIS →
Segment close rate, ASP, GM%, and GP dollars after fee by lender, plan, and advisor; every financed sale meets minimum GP $ after fees or requires manager approval before scheduling; celebrate financed close rate only alongside net economics.
#3

Jobs done, not billed — and AR aging that no one owns

 ▲ OPERATOR SIGNAL
TAG: OPERATOR SIGNAL — Especially on commercial & builder work

Backlog is not cash. In busy months, jobs get completed but not billed, change orders get executed without approval, retainage builds, and AR quietly slips past 45 and 60 days while everyone is heads-down on production. Cash quality decays before revenue quality shows any sign of stress.

In shoulder season, billing lag matters as much as sales lag — payroll and vendor terms don't wait for paperwork. This is a billing-process and ownership problem more than a customer problem. It wants one named owner working a weekly list with named customers, dates, and escalation — not “the office.”

Example: a $28,000 commercial job completed on Friday, billed Wednesday, invoiced at Net-30 — cash lands roughly 5 business days later than a same-day-billed baseline. Payroll runs Friday regardless. Do that across ten jobs a month and it becomes real working-capital drag.

DO THIS →
Weekly review of jobs done but not billed, AR over 45/60, retainage, and undocumented change orders; one accountable owner (not a department); identify which jobs are consuming capacity without turning into cash.

Channel-to-cash waterfall — where the July move gets absorbed

Channel activity — HARDI +6.7%, AHRI July shipments strong → Distributor revenue → Contractor purchases → Dispatch & Install execution → Callback absorption (the leak) → Billed revenue → Collected cash.

Every step introduces friction, but step 5 (callback absorption) is where profitable shoulder-season weeks quietly turn into break-even months. If your callback rate runs at 8% instead of an elite 3%, you are burning senior-tech hours on rework that could have been sold maintenance agreements or replacement diagnostics.

THE OPERATOR'S CHECKLIST FOR MONDAY MORNING
1. Pull the callback report by tech and crew — separate quick adjustments from real diagnostic or installation failures.
2. Review financing agreements with your controller to ensure dealer fees aren't silently eroding your gross margin targets.
3. Assign a single owner to track jobs completed but not billed and AR past 45 days.
4. Check your quote aging buckets — reprice any active replacement proposals sitting past 15 days.
